HUD's Fair Market Rents for Alaska, fiscal years 1983 to 2027: 30 counties, each with its rents by bedroom count and the FMR areas it has sat in. 3 of them have changed FMR area at least once (not counting 2006, when HUD renumbered every area). Its income limits are on its [limits page](../limits/Alaska.md), its tax credit housing on its [tax credits page](../lihtc/Alaska.md).

## Questions

**How has rent moved in Alaska?** The median two-bedroom rent of Alaska's 30 counties rose from $678 in FY1995 to $1,513 in FY2027, 123.1%; the nation's median county's rose 190.9% in the same years.

**Where in Alaska is rent dearest, and where cheapest?** In FY2027 a two-bedroom home rents dearest in [Aleutians West Census Area](./Alaska/Aleutians%20West%20Census%20Area.md), at $2,394 a month, and cheapest in [Kusilvak Census Area](./Alaska/Kusilvak%20Census%20Area.md), at $1,030. Every place is a row of the grid below, dearest first, its rent every fourth year since FY1995.

**Where in Alaska does rent take most of a low income?** Twelve months of the FY2026 two-bedroom rent are 30.1% of the very low income limit for four in the median of Alaska's counties (28.8% in the nation's median county); most in [Kodiak Island Borough](./Alaska/Kodiak%20Island%20Borough.md) (41.2%), least in [Denali Borough](./Alaska/Denali%20Borough.md) (19.5%).

**Has rent outrun income in Alaska?** From FY2006 to FY2026 the two-bedroom rent rose faster than the median family income in 13 of Alaska's 23 counties with both, and slower in 10.
