HUD's Fair Market Rents for Washington, fiscal years 1983 to 2027: 39 counties, each with its rents by bedroom count and the FMR areas it has sat in. 6 of them have changed FMR area at least once (not counting 2006, when HUD renumbered every area). Its income limits are on its [limits page](../limits/Washington.md), its tax credit housing on its [tax credits page](../lihtc/Washington.md).

## Questions

**How has rent moved in Washington?** The median two-bedroom rent of Washington's 39 counties rose from $455 in FY1995 to $1,467 in FY2027, 222.4%; the nation's median county's rose 190.9% in the same years.

**Where in Washington is rent dearest, and where cheapest?** In FY2027 a two-bedroom home rents dearest in [King County](./Washington/King%20County.md) and [Snohomish County](./Washington/Snohomish%20County.md), at $2,549 a month, and cheapest in [Ferry County](./Washington/Ferry%20County.md), at $1,058. Every place is a row of the grid below, dearest first, its rent every fourth year since FY1995.

**Where in Washington does rent take most of a low income?** Twelve months of the FY2026 two-bedroom rent are 33.1% of the very low income limit for four in the median of Washington's counties (28.8% in the nation's median county); most in [Thurston County](./Washington/Thurston%20County.md) (38.3%), least in [Ferry County](./Washington/Ferry%20County.md) (24.1%).

**Has rent outrun income in Washington?** From FY2006 to FY2026 the two-bedroom rent rose faster than the median family income in 30 of Washington's 39 counties with both, and slower in 9.
