HUD's Fair Market Rents for Nevada, fiscal years 1983 to 2027: 17 counties, each with its rents by bedroom count and the FMR areas it has sat in. 2 of them have changed FMR area at least once (not counting 2006, when HUD renumbered every area). Its income limits are on its [limits page](../limits/Nevada.md), its tax credit housing on its [tax credits page](../lihtc/Nevada.md).

## Questions

**How has rent moved in Nevada?** The median two-bedroom rent of Nevada's 17 counties rose from $562 in FY1995 to $1,327 in FY2027, 136.1%; the nation's median county's rose 190.9% in the same years.

**Where in Nevada is rent dearest, and where cheapest?** In FY2027 a two-bedroom home rents dearest in [Storey County](./Nevada/Storey%20County.md) and [Washoe County](./Nevada/Washoe%20County.md), at $1,837 a month, and cheapest in [Pershing County](./Nevada/Pershing%20County.md), at $1,014. Every place is a row of the grid below, dearest first, its rent every fourth year since FY1995.

**Where in Nevada does rent take most of a low income?** Twelve months of the FY2026 two-bedroom rent are 32.3% of the very low income limit for four in the median of Nevada's counties (28.8% in the nation's median county); most in [Clark County](./Nevada/Clark%20County.md) (39.5%), least in [Pershing County](./Nevada/Pershing%20County.md) (22.4%).

**Has rent outrun income in Nevada?** From FY2006 to FY2026 the two-bedroom rent rose faster than the median family income in 12 of Nevada's 17 counties with both, and slower in 5.
